Project context

Background & Objectives

This project involved the planning and financial structuring of a free-port industrial complex located in Ecuador. The development was designed to serve demand from logistics operators, light manufacturing firms, and commercial tenants looking for premium warehouse and office-warehouse space in a strategically located industrial zone.

The primary objective was to determine the financial viability of the project from an investor's perspective — specifically, whether a pre-sale financing model (common in Latin American real estate) could de-risk the construction phase by generating upfront capital through buyer deposits, reducing the required bank credit exposure.

The scope of work covered: (1) defining the full project structure using a five-phase WBS, (2) building a granular construction cost model across six asset categories, (3) computing break-even thresholds under two margin scenarios, and (4) projecting a five-year investor cash flow with NPV and WACC analysis.

Cost structure. The construction budget totalled $19.26M USD, dominated by office-warehouse units (44%) and standard warehouses (26%). Infrastructure — roads, fencing, and site services — represented 15% of costs, reflecting the greenfield nature of the development. Commercial areas and parking/workshop facilities accounted for the remaining 20%.

Break-even analysis. Under the conservative scenario (47% gross margin), the break-even point sits at 36.5 out of 37 total sellable units — a very tight margin of safety that underscores the importance of achieving near-full pre-sales before committing to construction financing. The optimistic scenario (65–85% margin), which reflects premium pricing and lower financing costs, brings the break-even down to 25.6 units, providing a more comfortable buffer and an ROI of 39.7%.

Investor cash flow. The model assumed an initial equity injection of $1.6M USD, with modest income in years 1–4 from pre-sales and early occupancy revenue. The bulk of the return — approximately $11.1M — is realized in Year 5 upon project completion and full handover. The resulting NPV of $4.49M at a 13.35% discount rate (WACC) confirms the project is financially viable for private investors under the pre-sale financing structure.

Conclusions & limitations

Conclusions

The financial analysis confirms that the industrial warehouse complex is a viable investment under both scenarios, though the conservative case leaves minimal room for error — nearly all 37 units must be sold to break even. This concentration risk highlights the critical importance of the pre-sale strategy: securing binding purchase commitments before mobilizing construction financing is essential to project success.

The pre-sale financing model itself is the key structural enabler. By converting buyer deposits (30–50% of unit value) into construction capital, the developer significantly reduces bank debt exposure and aligns investor risk with project milestones. This structure is directly analogous to pre-construction condominium financing used extensively in Canadian real estate markets.

Limitations: The model assumes stable construction costs and interest rates over a 5-year horizon, which may not hold in a high-inflation environment. Demand projections are not formally modelled — a full market absorption study would strengthen the feasibility case. Currency risk (USD-denominated costs vs. local revenues) was not explicitly hedged in this version of the model.

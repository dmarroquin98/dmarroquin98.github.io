Multi-Asset Portfolio Optimization —
Mean-Variance Analysis

Microsoft Excel · Excel Solver 9 ETFs · 4 asset classes n = 78 monthly observations (~6.5 years)

Construction and risk-adjusted performance evaluation of an Optimal Risky Portfolio using Mean-Variance optimization. The portfolio spans four asset classes — commodities, equities, bonds, and real estate investment trusts — and was benchmarked against a composite Canadian market index. Excel Solver was used to maximize the Sharpe ratio subject to a fully-invested, long-only constraint.

Methodology & context

Approach & Asset Universe

The portfolio was built using monthly price data across 9 ETFs covering four asset classes: commodities (GLD, WCOA.MI, COFF.L), North American equities (SPY, XIU.TO), Canadian fixed income (AGG, ZRR.TO, VAB.TO), and Canadian real estate (VRE.TO). All returns were calculated as Holding Period Yields (HPY), and risk premia were derived by subtracting the monthly risk-free rate.

The optimization objective was to maximize the Sharpe ratio — the ratio of portfolio excess return to portfolio standard deviation — subject to the constraint that all weights sum to 1 (fully invested, no short selling). This was solved using Excel's Solver add-in via the underlying mean-variance framework developed by Markowitz (1952).

Performance was then evaluated against a composite benchmark (Bloomberg Commodity Index + S&P/TSX Composite) using four standard risk-adjusted measures: Treynor ratio, Sharpe ratio, Jensen's alpha, and Information ratio.

Note on cumulative returns: The 78-month observation window spans approximately 6.5 years (~2018–2024), a period that includes significant commodity and equity appreciation. The 124% cumulative return is consistent with the portfolio's annualized return of 12.59% compounded over that period — it is not a single-year figure. The benchmark, by contrast, lost value over the same horizon (-8% cumulative), largely due to commodity index underperformance and rising rate headwinds on Canadian fixed income.

Optimal allocation. The Solver concentrated the portfolio in SPY (37.1%), GLD (31.7%), WCOA.MI (19.0%), and COFF.L (11.9%), with a negligible allocation to XIU.TO (0.2%) and zero weight to all bond and real estate ETFs. This result reflects the superior risk-adjusted performance of commodities and U.S. equities during the sample period — the optimizer found no marginal benefit in adding fixed income or REIT exposure given their lower Sharpe ratios and high correlations with existing holdings.

Diversification benefits. The correlation matrix reveals several meaningful diversification pairs: WCOA.MI is negatively correlated with VAB.TO (-0.33) and ZRR.TO (-0.27), and GLD shows near-zero correlation with VRE.TO (0.006). These low or negative correlations are what allow the portfolio to achieve a low beta (0.42) while maintaining strong absolute returns. Conversely, the high XIU.TO-SPY correlation (0.80) and ZRR.TO-VAB.TO correlation (0.87) explain why the optimizer treats these pairs as near-redundant.

Benchmark underperformance context. The benchmark produced a negative Sharpe ratio (-0.055) and cumulative loss of ~8% over 78 months, primarily driven by BCOM (Bloomberg Commodity Index) underperformance in the early portion of the sample and the negative impact of rising interest rates on the TSX Composite's rate-sensitive sectors. This made the outperformance comparison favorable but also reflects genuine period-specific tailwinds for the portfolio's commodity holdings.

Conclusions & limitations

Conclusions

The Mean-Variance optimization successfully identified a portfolio that outperformed the benchmark across all four risk-adjusted measures during the sample period. The concentration in commodities and U.S. equities was not assumed — it emerged from the data as the return-maximizing allocation given the observed covariance structure.

The low beta (0.42) is particularly notable: the portfolio achieved high absolute returns with substantially less systematic risk than the market, a combination that is genuinely difficult to achieve with a long-only constraint. This was made possible by the commodity allocations, which have historically low or negative correlation with broad equity indices during inflationary regimes.

Limitations: The optimization is backward-looking — it maximizes the in-sample Sharpe ratio and is subject to estimation error in expected returns and covariances. Out-of-sample performance may differ significantly. The model does not account for transaction costs, liquidity constraints, or taxes. The benchmark period (2018–2024) includes the COVID-19 commodity supercycle, which disproportionately favored the portfolio's commodity-heavy allocation and may not persist. A more robust implementation would apply rolling-window optimization and shrinkage estimators (e.g., Ledoit-Wolf) to reduce covariance estimation error.

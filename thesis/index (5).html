Factors Influencing E-Commerce
Purchase Intention During COVID-19

SPSS · SPSS AMOS · R n = 384 participants · Quito, Ecuador EFA → CFA → SEM pipeline 5 latent constructs · CFI 0.979

An empirical study investigating the factors that shaped online purchase behavior among consumers in Quito, Ecuador during the COVID-19 pandemic. Using a full EFA → CFA → SEM analytical pipeline grounded in the UTAUT 2 framework, the study tested which technology acceptance constructs — performance expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions — best explain habitual e-commerce usage and perceived service quality.

Research context & design

Background & Methodology

The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated e-commerce adoption globally, yet many consumers — particularly in emerging economies — remained reluctant to transact online. Ecuador's digital retail sector had grown substantially in the years before the pandemic (16M transactions, $1.29B in sales by 2018), but structural barriers including digital trust, payment infrastructure, and service quality continued to constrain growth. This study set out to identify which psychological and contextual factors most strongly predicted habitual e-commerce use among residents of Quito during the 2020–2021 period.

The theoretical foundation draws on the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT 2) by Venkatesh et al. (2012), extended with a Service Quality construct (CSB) adapted from the Three-Component Model of Rust & Oliver. Six domain experts validated the initial measurement instrument, which was administered as a five-point Likert-scale survey to 384 participants recruited from a digital database of online shoppers aged 18–65.

The analytical pipeline proceeded in four stages: (1) reliability analysis using Cronbach's alpha to identify and remove poorly performing items, (2) Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) in SPSS using Principal Axis Factoring with Varimax rotation to identify the latent factor structure, (3) Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) in SPSS AMOS using Maximum Likelihood estimation to test model fit and assess convergent validity via AVE and Composite Reliability, and (4) Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to test the hypothesized structural relationships between latent constructs. Multivariate normality was assessed using the Royston test in R.

Measurement model quality. All five retained constructs (ER, IS, CF, H, CSB) demonstrated good reliability with Cronbach's alpha above 0.86, and met convergent validity criteria with AVE values ranging from 0.619 to 0.766 and Composite Reliability above 0.83 across all constructs. The EFA identified a dominant first factor explaining 45.95% of total variance, with cumulative variance reaching 61.3% at four factors — a structure consistent with a general technology acceptance dimension underlying the specific constructs.

Model respecification. The initial CFA model (Model 1) showed marginal fit on RMSEA (0.055) and NFI (0.878). Guided by modification indices, correlated residuals were introduced between indicators sharing similar wording, yielding the final model (CFI 0.977, RMSEA 0.048). The SEM model, built on this CFA output with additional correlated errors within the ER and H constructs, achieved the best overall fit (CFI 0.979, RMSEA 0.046, AIC 301.4 — lowest across all models tested).

Structural hypotheses. Social Influence (IS → H, p < .001) and Facilitating Conditions (CF → H, p < .001) were both significant positive predictors of Habit — meaning that social norms around online shopping and the availability of supporting infrastructure (devices, internet, payment systems) were the primary drivers of habitual e-commerce behavior. Performance Expectancy (ER → H, p = .333) was not significant, suggesting that perceived usefulness alone does not translate into consistent online purchasing behavior in this population — a finding that diverges from results in more digitally mature markets and may reflect lower digital self-efficacy and trust among Quito consumers at the time of the study.

Conclusions & limitations

Conclusions

The study provides empirical evidence that social influence and structural facilitation — rather than perceived functional utility — are the primary levers driving habitual e-commerce adoption in Quito during the COVID-19 period. This has direct implications for digital marketing strategy: campaigns that emphasize social proof, peer behavior, and community adoption may be more effective than those focused on features or convenience alone. Ensuring access to reliable payment infrastructure and devices (facilitating conditions) is equally critical.

The non-significance of Performance Expectancy is a theoretically interesting departure from UTAUT 2 findings in developed-market contexts. It may reflect a saturation effect — by the time of the survey (mid-pandemic), most active online shoppers had already internalized the utility of e-commerce, making it a baseline expectation rather than a differentiating motivator for habit formation.

Limitations: The original research objective — modeling direct purchase intention (IC) — could not be fully realized because the IC construct was eliminated during CFA respecification due to insufficient observable indicators after item removal. The final model explains Habit and its relationship to perceived Service Quality (CSB), which are strong proxies for behavioral intention but not equivalent. Additionally, the non-probabilistic sampling method limits generalizability beyond the digitally accessible population in Quito. Future research should employ probabilistic sampling, a longer longitudinal design, and include a validated purchase intention scale with sufficient items to survive model respecification.
